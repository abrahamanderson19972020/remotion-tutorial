# World in Numbers – "How did the world reach 8 billion people?" (about 70 seconds)

Voiceover script. Timings are rough; we sync to the real voice in Module 6.

1. For almost all of human history, there were fewer than one billion people on Earth. (5 s)
2. Then, in about two hundred years, we became eight billion. How? (5 s)
3. The first billion took until around 1804. (4 s)
4. The second billion took one hundred and twenty-three years more, until 1927. (6 s)
5. Then the gaps got shorter. Three billion in 1960. Four billion in 1974. (6 s)
6. Five billion in 1987. Six billion in 1999. (5 s)
7. Since then, a new billion has arrived roughly every twelve years. (5 s)
8. The UN says we passed eight billion in November 2022. (5 s)
9. The main reason isn't more babies. It's fewer deaths: better food, clean water, vaccines and medicine. (8 s)
10. And growth is slowing. The UN expects the world to peak at about ten point three billion, in the mid-2080s. (8 s)
11. World in Numbers. Subscribe, and see the world in a new way. (4 s)

Data for the visuals: `public/data/population-milestones.csv` (UN estimates, rounded).
Facts to keep correct: billion milestones 1804, 1927, 1960, 1974, 1987, 1999, 2011, 2022 (UN; some revisions put 7 billion in 2012); 8 billion on 15 November 2022 (UN "Day of Eight Billion"); peak of about 10.3 billion in the mid-2080s (UN World Population Prospects 2024).
