# Daily Science & Engineering – "How does a jet engine work?" (about 70 seconds)

Voiceover script. Timings are rough; we sync to the real voice in Module 6.

1. A big airliner can weigh over 300 tonnes. So how do two engines push it into the sky? (5 s)
2. The answer fits in four words: suck, squeeze, bang, blow. (4 s)
3. Suck. A giant fan at the front pulls in a huge amount of air. (5 s)
4. Squeeze. Rows of spinning blades, called the compressor, squeeze that air into a much smaller space. (7 s)
5. Bang. Fuel is sprayed in and burned. The hot gas expands with enormous force. (6 s)
6. Blow. The hot gas rushes out of the back, and on its way it spins a turbine. (6 s)
7. The turbine is connected to the fan and the compressor by a shaft, so the engine keeps itself running. (7 s)
8. Here's the surprise. In most airliner engines, most of the push doesn't come from the hot gas at all. (7 s)
9. Most of the air from the fan flows around the core. It's called bypass air, and it makes most of the thrust, quietly and efficiently. (9 s)
10. Push air backwards, and the plane moves forwards. That's Newton's third law, at about 900 kilometres per hour. (8 s)
11. Daily Science & Engineering. Subscribe to see how the world works. (4 s)

Facts to keep correct: "over 300 tonnes" (e.g. a Boeing 777-300ER can take off at about 350 t); in high-bypass turbofans the bypass air produces most of the thrust; typical airliner cruise speed is about 900 km/h.
